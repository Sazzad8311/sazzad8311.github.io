About

I am a Graduate Research Assistant and M.S. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Lamar University. My research covers quantum machine learning, power side channels and energy measurement on GPUs, and reliable, explainable models for medical image analysis. I run my experiments on H100 and A100 GPUs on the Lonestar6 system at the Texas Advanced Computing Center.

Before joining Lamar in January 2026, I was a Lecturer in the Department of Information and Communication Engineering at Daffodil International University, where I taught undergraduate courses and supervised research and capstone projects from 2022 to 2025. I hold a B.Sc. in Electronics and Telecommunication Engineering from Chittagong University of Engineering and Technology (CUET).

Research

Benchmarking hybrid quantum models

I benchmark quantum image dataset transform (QIDT) pre-processing across hybrid quantum architectures, including QCNN, QuanvNet, and QViT. Each model is evaluated on accuracy together with throughput, GPU power, and training energy, so that predictive gains can be weighed against their computing cost.

Related: IEEE QCE 2026 paper

Power side channels on datacenter GPUs

This work asks whether GPU power telemetry can reveal the architecture of a neural network running on the device. It covers vision and language models, and pays particular attention to whether the power measurements themselves are correct.

Related: TACCSTER 2026 poster, SC26 poster, PULSE code

Reliable and explainable medical image analysis

Building on my earlier chest X-ray classification work for pneumonia and COVID-19, I study calibration, selective prediction, and explanation auditing with Grad-CAM, LIME, and SHAP, so that a diagnostic model’s confidence and reasoning can be examined.

Related: IEEE CSDE 2026 paper, Sensors International article, IPRECON 2021 paper
